    How the totals are worked outThe same arithmetic as the study
    • Money in (buying + building, one-time) is the one-time items added up, including buying the property at an assumed $3.0M plus fees and closing.
    • Hall bookings count each hall each time it is rented, the way the study counts its events: both halls on the same day count as two, one side as one.
    • Money coming in each year is hall bookings a year times the money from each booking, plus the rents and other income.
    • Money going out each year is hall bookings a year times the cost of each booking, plus the year-round hall costs and the other yearly costs.
    • Profit each year is money coming in minus money going out, before any loan payments.
    • Years to earn back the money put in is the money in (buying and building) divided by the profit each year.
    • Each line stands on its own: changing one does not change the others. In real life some move together, for example cheaper building work also lowers design and permit costs, and higher prices also mean higher card fees.

            WORDS USED ON THIS PAGE

            What the words mean

            Worst, average and best case
            Likely outcomes, not extremes. Every input (costs, rents, bookings, prices) has a researched range, and thousands of simulated futures let each land anywhere in it. Worst: only 1 in 10 futures do worse. Average: the typical future. Best: only 1 in 10 do better.
            Money in (buying + building, one-time)
            Buying the property at an assumed $3.0M plus fees, closing costs, surveys and inspections, then repairs, building work, finishing the inside, design and permits, a cushion for surprises, the cost of signing tenants, and holding the building while work goes on.
            Money coming in each year
            Rent from tenants plus sales from the halls where the owners run them, once the building is open.
            Money going out each year
            The yearly running costs: property tax, insurance, upkeep, management, a repairs reserve, empty space and unpaid rent, and the halls’ staff, food and other costs.
            Profit each year
            Money coming in minus money going out, before any loan payments.
            Years to earn back the money put in
            The one-time money in (buying and building) divided by the profit each year. Fewer years is better.
            Profit each year for every $100 spent
            The same idea the other way round: $10 a year for every $100 spent earns the money back in 10 years.
            Typical investment property
            A finished, rented property like this one usually sells for about 12 years of its profit. A plan that earns back faster creates value; one that takes longer is worth less than it cost to build.
            The 10-year goal
            This study’s goal: earn back the money put in within 10 years, which is $10 a year for every $100 spent.
            Your test numbers
            Numbers you type in “Try your own numbers”. They stay on your screen for this visit only, are not saved and are not part of the study.
            Owner-run
            Run by the owners’ own operating company instead of being rented to another business.
            Tenant’s share of property costs
            On top of rent, tenants pay their share of property tax, insurance and upkeep of shared areas.
            Land lease
            A business rents only the land, builds its own building on it and pays rent for the land.
            Transfer girder
            A deep steel beam that holds up the roof where a column used to stand, so the column can come out.
            SF
            Square feet.
