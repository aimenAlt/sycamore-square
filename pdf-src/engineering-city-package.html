Sycamore Square — Engineering Review and City Pre-Application Package

Public version · 18 Oct 2026

Purpose

This package asks a licensed engineering firm to confirm the concept plans, and the City of Memphis to say at a pre-application meeting what approvals the plan needs. The drawings come from county GIS data and the building's 1997 tenant plans, not a survey. They show what the owners intend to build and where the open questions are. They are not construction, permit or conveyance documents.

The plan turns the existing 127,634 SF single-story building at 1699–1735 Sycamore View Road into five parts:

  • Two event halls, Hall A and Hall B, with kitchens and a shared dining room
  • A kids' recreation center
  • Small-shop retail
  • Flex space
  • An optional fuel station with a convenience store on the frontage

We need three things from this round:

  1. From the engineers: a fee proposal and a short written review that confirms or corrects the layout, flood strategy, parking and site access.
  2. From the City: the approval path for each use and each parcel, the flood rules that apply, and the studies the City will require.
  3. From both: a list of anything that would stop the plan or change its cost by more than 10%.

Before sending: what the owner adds

This public version leaves out three things that only the owner can supply. Add them to the copy that goes out:

  • A cover letter with the owner's contact details, how to arrange access to the building, and the deadline: the property is offered by online auction on 2–4 Nov 2026, so fee proposals and the City meeting are needed before then.
  • The 1997 Sitel tenant plans, sheets A2.1 and A2.2. Every column position, the column grid, the sprinkler riser room and the 1-hour wall in this package come from them. They are not public, so they are not on the website.
  • Photographs of the existing building: interior, roof and rooftop units, the rear truck docks and the parking lot. The website shows only concept images, which are not the building as it stands.

Proposed program

The whole existing building is reused and its outer walls do not move. A new 4,000 SF convenience store is the only new building, and it is optional. Areas are gross planning budgets; net usable areas come after a measured interior survey.

The halls run three ways. One client can book Hall A and Hall B together for a single wedding, with guests split between two rooms. Two unrelated events can run at once, each with its own entrance, kitchen and staff. Or both halls can open to each other as one coordinated event. A movable wall in each main room also lets either room split for two smaller events of 150–350 guests.

Separate arrivals. Hall A guests arrive from the east lot through the east façade. Hall B guests arrive from the south lot through the south-facing wall of the B foyer. The two arrivals are on separate façades, so two events can arrive at the same time without crossing paths.

Column-free main rooms. Each 11,000 SF main room has 2 existing roof columns (1997 drawings, about 44.5 ft grid). The owners want column-free rooms, so the plan removes all four with steel transfer girders of about 88 ft span. The structural question below asks whether that works and what it costs.

Fuel has two candidate locations; no decision is made yet.

  • F1, frontage (preferred): a 72 × 40 ft canopy with six fueling positions and a 40 × 20 ft tank area on the 1717 frontage, about 91 ft from Sycamore View. It is reached from the existing throat drive, and the store sits beside it on the main parcel. It sits on the former quick-lube pad.
  • F2, 5800 Summer (fallback): store and canopy as one block on the pad parcel beside Summer Avenue, outside the city flood layer as drawn; confirm against FEMA's map.
  • No fuel: the frontage stays parking and landscaping.

An alternative layout is under study with the halls at the north end of the building and the shops at the south end. In it, Hall A enters from the north lot off Pleasant View Road and Hall B from the east lot, and the kids' center sits between the halls and the shops. It uses the same program and the same gross areas. It is shown on sheets ALT-1 to ALT-3 in a separate PDF, Sycamore_Square_Alternative_Layout_Halls_North.pdf.

  • the residential edge to the west of the site
  • fire access to both hall entrances

Next steps

Order the elevation certificate and survey first: the certificate should confirm the floor sits above the flood level, and every other engineer needs the survey. The property is offered by online auction on 2–4 Nov 2026 and is sold as-is, so the survey, elevation certificate, Phase I and City answers should all be in hand before any binding commitment. Start all four now.

  1. Send this package and the files above to two or three civil engineering firms that work in Memphis, with the owner's cover letter, and ask each for a fee proposal for the reviews above.
  2. Order the ALTA survey with topography and an elevation certificate now, for delivery before any binding commitment. Order the Phase I environmental assessment the same day.
  3. Ask a structural engineer to inspect the building in the next few days: confirm the roof framing, the column and footing sizes and the clear height in both halls, and give a cost for removing the four interior columns.
  4. Ask DPD now for a pre-application meeting, naming the departments listed above. Send the drawings at least a week ahead.
  5. Hold the meeting. Write the City's answer next to each question, and ask staff to confirm the notes by email.
  6. Request the written zoning verification letters and the floodplain administrator's method for the 50% test now, without waiting for the meeting.
  7. Bring the survey, flood answer, City answers and engineering review together into revision R5 of the plans, and update both feasibility studies with real costs.
  8. Decide whether to proceed only once the survey, flood, City and engineering answers are in hand.
