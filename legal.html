LEGAL & REGULATORY FINDINGS · OCTOBER 2026

Flood and zoning decide whether the plan works

Most of the building sits in a FEMA 100-year flood zone. No source has yet confirmed that event halls, fuel sales or supervised childcare are allowed under the site's CMU-3 zoning. Everything here comes from public records and desk research.

Not legal advice. Counsel, a licensed surveyor and the City of Memphis must confirm each item before closing.

01 · WHAT STANDS OUT

Six things to know first

1 · ENVIRONMENTAL

The frontage used to be an oil-change shop

County survey data from a 2017 aerial flight records a 2,086 SF building on the 1717 Sycamore View frontage, coded "mini lube" and built in 1981. Current imagery shows no roof; a bare or debris-covered pad remains.

  • Quick-lube shops typically had used-oil tanks, service pits and hydraulic lifts, all common sources of soil and groundwater contamination.
  • The pad sits where the preferred fuel canopy would go.
  • A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment, done to the federal "all appropriate inquiries" standard (ASTM E1527-21) within a year before closing, is what generally preserves a buyer's defenses against inheriting cleanup liability.

2 · FLOOD

Three-quarters of the building is in the 100-year floodplain

A renovation reaching 50% of the building's value forces the whole building up to flood code. A surveyor's elevation certificate could instead show it is high enough to be removed from the flood zone.

3 · BOUNDARY

The broker's boundary does not match the records

The four recorded parcels, frontage included, total 11.88 acres, not the 11.72 advertised. The broker's straight line takes in about 1,089 SF of the neighboring 5828 Summer lot, so our plans ignore it and follow the recorded lines. The contract must name all four parcels in full.

4 · BUILDING HISTORY

Its call-center past may cut conversion costs

The 1997 build-out plans show an existing sprinkler riser room and a 1-hour fire-rated wall dividing the building in half. Both help with tenant separation and the code upgrades an event-hall conversion triggers.

5 · ZONING

Memphis is rewriting its zoning code

A 2025/2026 update to the Unified Development Code is in progress, so use permissions and parking rules could change during the deal. Request the zoning letter under the current code, and track the update.

6 · ACCESS

Summer Avenue is a federal highway (US-70)

The state, not just the city, controls driveway changes on that side of the property.

02 · CONFIRMED FROM PUBLIC RECORDS

What the records show

From Shelby County / City of Memphis GIS and the broker's offering memorandum. Each item still needs certification by a surveyor or the city.

Zoning
All three acquisition parcels are zoned CMU-3 (commercial mixed use). Land behind the building is zoned residential or agricultural (CA, CMU-2 with single-family use, R-6 / R-8).
Why it matters: use permissions are not yet confirmed, and the residential edge usually brings buffer or screening duties along the rear.
Flood zone
About 74% of the building footprint and about 4.0 acres of the site lie in FEMA Zone AE on FIRM panel 47157C0311G. The county property record also lists flood zone AE.
Why it matters: federally backed loans require flood insurance here. The city layer is a copy, so the effective FEMA map must be confirmed.
The 50% rule
Under the National Flood Insurance Program, improvements costing 50% or more of the structure's market value (not the land) make a building "substantially improved". It must then comply with current flood rules.
Why it matters: a full event conversion will very likely cross 50%, so the building would have to be elevated or flood-proofed. The site's flood elevation is not published.
Possible way out
If an elevation certificate shows the building's lowest adjacent ground is above the flood elevation, FEMA can issue a Letter of Map Amendment (LOMA) removing it from the zone.
Why it matters: it is the cheapest, fastest test on the whole diligence list. Do it first.
Parcels and acreage
088066 00027 (9.69 ac, main building), 088066 00020 (5800 Summer, 1.33 ac) and 088066 00021 (5810 Summer, 0.47 ac) total 11.50 ac. With the 0.38 ac frontage (088066 00024), the four recorded parcels total 11.88 ac; the broker states 11.72 ac.
Why it matters: the 0.16 ac difference must be reconciled by survey before price and appraisal are settled.
Frontage parcel
The 1717 Sycamore View frontage is a separate record, 088066 00024 (0.38 ac). The broker's straight boundary line overlaps about 1,089 SF of the adjoining 5828 Summer record; the plans use the recorded lines instead.
Why it matters: the contract and legal description must name the frontage, and survey and title must resolve the overlap.
Former quick-lube
County planimetric data (2017 flight) records a 2,086 SF "mini lube" building on the 1717 frontage, built in 1981. Current imagery shows no roof.
Why it matters: former oil-change sites can carry buried tanks, waste oil or soil contamination. A Phase I should cover this pad before closing.

03 · FOUND, NOT YET VERIFIED

Likely true, still to confirm

From desk research and general rules. Each needs confirmation from title, the state or the city.

Selling the pads separately

5800 and 5810 Summer have their own tax parcel numbers, but that alone does not prove they can be sold or financed separately. Recorded plats, title and any reciprocal access and parking agreements decide it.

Access and parking agreements

Shared driveways and parking with the adjoining retail parcels are likely governed by recorded easements or a reciprocal easement agreement. These transfer with title and can limit how lots and drives are reconfigured.

Fuel station rules

Tennessee's UST division requires notice before installation, leak detection, spill and overfill protection, and proof of financial responsibility. A ground lease to a fuel operator does not automatically remove the landowner's environmental exposure; the lease must allocate it.

State highway access

Summer Avenue is US-70, a state route. A new or changed driveway there likely needs a Tennessee Department of Transportation permit.

Change of building use

Converting a former office and call center to event halls is a change to an assembly occupancy. That triggers current fire exit, sprinkler, fire alarm, restroom and ADA requirements; the existing sprinkler room and rated wall can help.

04 · OPEN QUESTIONS

What no source has answered yet

The largest unknown is whether the planned uses are allowed in CMU-3. The full code text could not be retrieved, and the 2025–26 update drafts are not adopted law.

  • Are event halls, indoor recreation, supervised childcare and fuel sales permitted by right in CMU-3, or do any need a special use permit? Are there setbacks from the residential edge?

    SETTLED BYA written zoning verification letter from Memphis Planning and Development, for each parcel and use.

  • Does supervised care of wedding guests' children need a Tennessee child care license? Do exemptions apply when parents stay on site?

    SETTLED BYA written determination from Tennessee DHS for the actual operating model.

  • Can the public play center offer restricted-access sessions, such as family-only or private-group hours? (Private event bookings set their own guest arrangements.)

    SETTLED BYCounsel's review of state and local public-accommodation law.

  • What parking minimums apply to each use, and are shared-parking reductions available?

    SETTLED BYThe code's parking section, confirmed in the zoning letter, plus a surveyed stall count. The broker's 909 is unverified.

  • What is the base flood elevation, and does local floodplain law count improvements cumulatively over several years?

    SETTLED BYAn elevation certificate and a meeting with the Memphis floodplain administrator.

05 · CONTRACT CONTINGENCIES

Build these into the purchase contract

A due-diligence period long enough to finish each item, with the right to exit if any fails. Start with flood and zoning.

  1. 01ALTA boundary and topographic survey, reconciling the 11.88 ac of record against the 11.72 ac advertised, and the frontage line
  2. 02Title commitment, deeds, recorded plats, easements and any access and parking agreements
  3. 03FEMA elevation certificate and the effective flood map, plus a LOMA if the building sits above flood elevation
  4. 04Floodplain administrator meeting on how substantial improvement will be measured
  5. 05Zoning verification letter for every planned use and parcel
  6. 06Phase I Environmental Site Assessment, with specific attention to the former quick-lube
  7. 07Property condition, roof, structural and MEP assessment, including the sprinkler and electrical systems
