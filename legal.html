LEGAL & REGULATORY FINDINGS · OCTOBER 2026

Flood and zoning decide whether the plan works

Most of the building sits in a FEMA 100-year flood zone. No source has yet confirmed that event halls, fuel sales or supervised childcare are allowed under the site's commercial mixed-use (CMU-3) zoning. Everything here comes from public records and desk research.

Not legal advice. Counsel, a licensed surveyor and the City of Memphis must confirm each item before any binding commitment.

01 · WHAT STANDS OUT

Six things to know first

1 · ENVIRONMENTAL

The frontage used to be an oil-change shop

County survey data from a 2017 aerial flight records a 2,086 SF building on the 1717 Sycamore View frontage, coded "mini lube" and built in 1981. It was demolished, foundation and footings included, under permit DEM-24-000379 (31 Oct 2024), but what lies below ground is still unknown.

  • Quick-lube shops typically had used-oil tanks, service pits and hydraulic lifts, all common sources of soil and groundwater contamination.
  • The pad sits where the preferred fuel canopy would go.
  • A Phase I environmental report (a check of records and the site for past contamination), done to the federal "all appropriate inquiries" standard (ASTM E1527-21) within a year before buying, is what generally protects a buyer from inheriting cleanup bills. Counsel should confirm the scope.

2 · FLOOD

Three-quarters of the building is in the 100-year floodplain

A renovation reaching 50% of the building's value, about $1.5M of building work, forces the whole building up to flood code. Laser elevation data puts the ground along about 85% of the walls at 250–252 ft, so the floor likely already meets the City's rule of 250 ft, two feet above FEMA's 248 ft flood level; a survey must confirm. The low rear dock area, at 247.7–248.8 ft, makes it uncertain that FEMA would take the building off the flood map by letter (a LOMA).

3 · BOUNDARY

The broker's site line crosses a neighbor's lot

The four parcels, frontage included, total 11.72 acres on the county assessor's records (9.533 + 1.353 + 0.476 + 0.362), matching the broker; the county's digital map outlines add up to 11.88 acres, and a full boundary survey (ALTA) settles the difference. Laid over the county map, the broker's straight line takes in about 1,100 SF of the neighboring 5828 Summer lot (survey to confirm), so the plans ignore it and follow the recorded lines. The frontage is a separate parcel, and any purchase must name all four parcels in full.

4 · BUILDING HISTORY

Its call-center past may cut conversion costs

The 1997 build-out plans show an existing sprinkler riser room and a 1-hour fire-rated wall about 270 ft north of the south wall, between the two tenant areas shown on those plans. Both help with tenant separation and the code upgrades an event-hall conversion triggers.

5 · ZONING

Memphis is rewriting its zoning code

A 2025/2026 update to the Unified Development Code is in progress, so use permissions and parking rules could change during the deal. Request the zoning letter under the current code, and track the update.

6 · ACCESS

Summer Avenue is US-70, a state route

The state, not just the city, controls driveway changes on that side of the property. The state transportation department (TDOT) is also widening Summer Avenue, including the Fletcher Creek bridge, through mid-2028.

02 · CONFIRMED FROM PUBLIC RECORDS

What the records show

From Shelby County / City of Memphis maps (GIS), the county assessor and permit records, FEMA and U.S. Geological Survey flood and elevation data, and the broker's marketing materials. Each item still needs certification by a surveyor or the city.

Zoning
Three of the four parcels (088066 00027, 00020 and 00021) are zoned CMU-3 (commercial mixed use) per county maps; the zoning of the frontage parcel (00024) is still to confirm. Land to the west, behind the building, is zoned residential or agricultural (CA, CMU-2 with single-family use, R-6 / R-8). Parts of the site fall under the city's extra flood rules (the floodplain overlay, UDC 8.8), and the site is very likely inside the Fletcher Creek Overlay (UDC 8.9), which adds storm-water and floor-height rules.
Why it matters: use permissions are not yet confirmed, and the residential edge usually brings buffer or screening duties along the rear.
Flood zone
About 74% of the building footprint lies in FEMA Zone AE, the 100-year floodplain; the rest is in the lower-risk shaded Zone X. The current FEMA map panel is 47157C0311G (6 Feb 2013), with Fletcher Creek as the flood source and no floodway (the creek channel area where building is most restricted) on the site. Confirmed on FEMA's online flood map data, 18 Oct 2026.
Why it matters: federally regulated lenders must require flood insurance on loans secured by buildings in this zone (42 U.S.C. 4012a (opens in a new tab)).
Flood elevation
FEMA's 100-year flood level next to the building (the base flood elevation, or BFE) is 248 ft above sea level. Laser elevation data flown by the U.S. Geological Survey in March 2017 puts the ground at 250–252 ft along about 85% of the walls, and at 247.7–248.8 ft along about 240 ft of the rear (west) truck-dock area.
Why it matters: in the floodplain overlay, Memphis requires the floor of a new or substantially improved building to sit 24 inches above the BFE: 250 ft here. The floor likely already meets that if it sits at or above the surrounding ground. A survey must confirm it.
The 50% rule
Under the National Flood Insurance Program, work costing 50% or more of what the building (not the land) is worth makes it "substantially improved". It must then meet today's flood rules. At the assessor's 2026 building value of $2,994,800, the threshold is about $1.5M of building work, with site work excluded; an independent appraisal can set a higher value.
Why it matters: a full event conversion will very likely cross 50%. The whole building would then have to meet current flood rules, including the 250 ft floor (24 inches above the 248 ft BFE); new structures or expansions under the Fletcher Creek Overlay likely need 30 inches. If the floor falls short, elevating or flood-proofing can dominate the budget.
Possible way out
FEMA can issue a Letter of Map Amendment (LOMA) removing the building from the flood zone if a surveyor's elevation certificate shows the lowest ground touching it is at or above the 248 ft BFE (44 CFR 65.5 (opens in a new tab)). Two nearby properties had structures removed this way in 2021, and a third was confirmed already outside the zone as mapped.
Why it matters: the rear dock area, at 247.7–248.8 ft, probably sets the lowest adjacent ground, so a LOMA is uncertain; the likelier result is a survey showing the floor already meets 250 ft. FEMA has 60 days to rule on a complete request and can take 60 more (44 CFR 70.4 (opens in a new tab)), so a LOMA cannot arrive before the auction. The elevation certificate itself is quick: do it first.
Parcels and acreage
088066 00027 (9.533 ac, main building), 088066 00020 (5800 Summer, 1.353 ac) and 088066 00021 (5810 Summer, 0.476 ac) total 11.362 ac. With the 0.362 ac frontage (088066 00024), the four total 11.724 ac on the county assessor's 2026 records, matching the broker's 11.72 ac; the county's digital map outlines add up to 11.88 ac. The earlier offering memorandum listed only three parcel IDs; the updated auction teaser lists all four.
Why it matters: the 0.16 ac gap between the county map outlines (11.88 ac) and the assessor and broker acreage (11.72 ac) should be settled by a full boundary survey (ALTA).
Frontage parcel
The 1717 Sycamore View frontage is a separate record, 088066 00024 (0.362 ac per the assessor), and it is also marketed on its own. Laid over the county map, the broker's straight boundary line overlaps about 1,100 SF of the adjoining 5828 Summer record (survey to confirm); the plans use the recorded lines instead.
Why it matters: the legal description of any purchase must name the frontage explicitly, and survey and title must resolve the overlap.
Former quick-lube
County mapping data from a 2017 aerial flight records a 2,086 SF "mini lube" building on the 1717 frontage, built in 1981. It was demolished, foundation and footings included, under permit DEM-24-000379, dated 31 Oct 2024; the assessor's record dates it 1 Nov 2024.
Why it matters: former oil-change sites can carry buried tanks, waste oil or soil contamination. What lies below ground is still unknown, so a Phase I environmental report should cover this pad before any binding commitment.

03 · FOUND, NOT YET VERIFIED

Likely true, still to confirm

From desk research and general rules. Each needs confirmation from title, the state or the city.

Selling the pads separately

5800 and 5810 Summer have their own tax parcel numbers, but that alone does not prove they can be sold or financed separately. Recorded plats, title and any reciprocal access and parking agreements decide it. The seller already markets 1717 Sycamore View and 5810 Summer on their own.

Access and parking agreements

Shared driveways and parking with the adjoining retail parcels are likely governed by recorded easements or a reciprocal easement agreement. These transfer with title and can limit how lots and drives are reconfigured.

Fuel station rules

Tennessee's underground storage tank (UST) division requires notice before installation, leak detection, spill and overfill protection, and proof of financial responsibility. A ground lease to a fuel operator does not automatically remove the landowner's environmental exposure; the lease must allocate it.

Fletcher Creek Overlay

The site is very likely inside the Fletcher Creek Overlay (UDC 8.9). This extra layer of city rules requires new buildings or additions, such as a fuel station store, to have floors 30 inches above the nearest flood level, and staff reports (P.D. 21-22 (opens in a new tab), S 15-012 (opens in a new tab)) treat holding back storm water on site (detention) as mandatory. The City should confirm whether the parcels are inside it.

State highway access

Summer Avenue is US-70, a state route. A new or changed driveway there likely needs a Tennessee Department of Transportation permit. TDOT's widening of Summer Avenue runs through mid-2028.

Change of building use

Converting a former office and call center to event halls changes it to an "assembly" use, a place where crowds gather. That triggers today's fire exit, sprinkler, fire alarm, restroom and accessibility (ADA) requirements; the existing sprinkler room and fire-rated wall can help.

04 · OPEN QUESTIONS

What no source has answered yet

The largest unknown is whether the planned uses are allowed in CMU-3. The full code text could not be retrieved, and the 2025–26 update drafts are not adopted law.

  • Are event halls, indoor recreation, supervised childcare and fuel sales permitted by right in CMU-3, or do any need a special use permit? Are there setbacks from the residential edge?

    SETTLED BYA written zoning verification letter from Memphis Planning and Development, for each parcel and use.

  • Does supervised care of wedding guests' children need a Tennessee child care license? Do exemptions apply when parents stay on site?

    SETTLED BYA written determination from Tennessee DHS for the actual operating model.

  • Can the public play center offer restricted-access sessions, such as family-only or private-group hours? (Private event bookings set their own guest arrangements.)

    SETTLED BYCounsel's review of state and local public-accommodation law.

  • What parking minimums apply to each use, and are shared-parking reductions available?

    SETTLED BYThe code's parking section, confirmed in the zoning letter, plus a surveyed stall count. The broker's 909 is unverified.

  • Does the change to event use put the building in a higher earthquake-safety class? Under the 2021 building code, assembly rooms of more than 300 people each that together hold more than 2,500 people make a building "Risk Category III", which can require earthquake strengthening of the existing building in Memphis. With table-and-chair layouts the two halls hold about 1,470 people; chairs-only ceremony seating would cross the line.

    SETTLED BYA structural engineer's code review and the City's written answer at the pre-application meeting, including whether capping each hall's posted capacity keeps the building in its current class.

  • The base flood elevation is 248 ft (FEMA). Still open: does local floodplain law count improvements cumulatively over several years, and does the Fletcher Creek Overlay (UDC 8.9) apply?

    SETTLED BYA meeting with the Memphis floodplain administrator.

TERMS EXPLAINED

What the technical words mean

SF, ac
Square feet and acres. One acre is 43,560 SF.
Zoning, CMU-3
Zoning decides which uses are allowed on a lot. CMU-3 is the Memphis commercial mixed-use district these parcels are in.
100-year floodplain, FEMA Zone AE
Land with a 1% chance of flooding in any year, as mapped by FEMA. Federally backed loans there require flood insurance.
50% rule (substantial improvement)
If the work costs 50% or more of the building’s value, the whole building must meet current flood rules.
Base flood elevation (BFE)
The water level FEMA maps for the flood with a 1% chance in any year. Here it is 248 ft above sea level (NAVD88).
Elevation certificate, LOMA
A surveyor’s record of the building’s elevations, including the lowest ground touching it (the lowest adjacent grade). If that ground is at or above the base flood elevation, FEMA can issue a Letter of Map Amendment (LOMA) taking the building out of the flood zone.
Due diligence
The checks a buyer makes before committing to buy, such as survey, title, environmental and zoning.
ALTA survey
A detailed boundary survey of the kind title companies and lenders rely on.
Phase I
An environmental review of records and the site, looking for past contamination.
Overlay district
An extra layer of zoning rules on top of the base zoning, such as the Memphis floodplain overlay (UDC 8.8) or the Fletcher Creek Overlay (UDC 8.9).
C-store, QSR
A convenience store, and a quick-service (fast-food) restaurant.
DXF, GLB, BLEND
Technical file types: a CAD drawing, a 3D model and a Blender scene. They need special software to open.
