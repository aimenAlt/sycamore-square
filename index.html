01 · THE SITE

A big box on a busy corner, with room to divide

The building fronts a large east parking field, with frontage reaching Sycamore View Road between two neighboring retail buildings and two pad parcels on Summer Avenue.

PARCELS

  • 1735 Sycamore View Rd · 088066 00027 · 9.533 ac
  • 5800 Summer Ave · 088066 00020 · 1.353 ac
  • 5810 Summer Ave · 088066 00021 · 0.476 ac
  • 1717 Sycamore View frontage · 088066 00024 · 0.362 ac
  • Total 11.72 ac on the county assessor's 2026 records; the county's digital map outlines add up to 11.88 ac. The frontage has the same owner of record as the other three; it and 5810 Summer are also marketed on their own. Title and survey to confirm.

BUILDING

  • Built 1984; fitted out as a call center in the late 1990s. County assessor: 125,235 SF, grade C
  • Interior roof columns about 44.5 ft apart in the south half; the two inside each planned main room come out, with long steel beams (transfer girders) added to carry the roof
  • A 1-hour rated wall about 270 ft north of the south wall separates the two 1997 tenant areas
  • From the 1997 Sitel tenant plans; field measurement required.

ACCESS

  • Two drives on Pleasant View Rd (D1, D2), one on Sycamore View Rd (D3) and one on Summer Ave (D4), per site plan C-1
  • The state (TDOT) is widening Summer Ave, including the Fletcher Creek bridge, through mid-2028

NOT PART OF THE SITE

  • Family Dollar (1745 Sycamore View, 088066 00025C) and the detached brick retail building (5828 Summer, 088066 00022C) remain with their owners.

03 · LAYOUT ALTERNATIVE

Halls north, stores south

An alternative under study swaps the two ends of the building. The event complex moves to the north end and the shops to the south end, nearer Summer Avenue. The program, the areas and the cost stay about the same.

HOW IT WORKS

  • Hall A sits at the north end, with its foyer on the north façade; guests arrive from the north lot off the Pleasant View Road drives
  • Hall B sits below it; guests arrive from the east lot through their own foyer on the east façade
  • The kitchens, service room and dining room sit between the two halls; the kitchens take deliveries from the rear drive
  • The indoor playground stays in the middle of the building, with its own public entrance on the east façade
  • Shops wrap the east and south façades at the south end; flex space takes the rear (west) side

WHAT CHANGES

  • The two hall arrivals use different façades, north and east
  • The shops move nearer Summer Avenue and its drive
  • The two hall doors end up about 207 ft apart (121 ft in the current layout), but more of the hall space sits in the mapped flood zone (about 71% against 56%)
  • The 1997 drawings do not cover the north end, so its column positions are assumed until measured. Columns inside the main rooms would come out with long steel beams (transfer girders), as in the current layout.

STATUS

  • Same program and a similar cost; under study
  • A side-by-side comparison with the current layout (sheet ALT-3) is in the PDF

05 · THE EVENT COMPLEX

Two halls that work apart or together

Hall A and Hall B can host one large wedding across two rooms, two separate events at once, or one coordinated event.

  • 2 × 11,000 SF

    Column-free main rooms, 132 × 83 ft each. The two existing roof columns in each room (1997 drawings, about 44.5 ft apart) come out, with long steel beams (transfer girders) added to carry the roof; a structural engineer confirms before any binding commitment, and the cost is in the feasibility numbers

  • 4 × 700 SF

    Four family suites, two per hall, each with a private bath and hair, makeup and nail prep

  • 2 kitchens

    About 1,950 SF each, with rear receiving, a direct serving wall into their own hall and a controlled door between them

  • 2,400 SF

    Reservable dining room, plus a guest gallery linking the two foyers

The two halls arrive on different sides: Hall A on the east face, Hall B on the south side.

One wedding, both halls

One family books Hall A and Hall B

Guests can use both rooms. Each hall has its own entrance, foyer, restrooms and family suites, so neither room passes through the other. Hall A guests arrive from the east lot and Hall B guests from the south lot, on different sides of the building.

  • The private child and family zone opens off the event complex, so wedding guests reach it without crossing the public play lobby.
  • One caterer can run both kitchens through the controlled connecting door.
  • Each hall can have its own service team and schedule.

Two independent events

Two unrelated bookings at once

Each hall works as a complete venue: its own entrance, foyer, restrooms, suites, kitchen and staff.

  • Arrivals stay apart: Hall A guests come from the east lot, Hall B guests from the south lot.
  • Food reaches each hall from its own kitchen, never through the other event.
  • The dining room goes to one event, or to both in separate time slots.
  • Deliveries and waste stay on the rear service drive, away from guests.

One coordinated event

One large linked event

The guest gallery opens between the two foyers under one management plan. Both kitchens serve together and the dining room becomes a shared space.

  • Both entrances and every required exit stay in use.
  • It is a linked complex, not one unobstructed ballroom.

The 18,000 SF indoor playground (soft play, climbing, party rooms) has its own public entrance and also serves wedding families through a supervised private zone. Guest capacity, fixture counts and childcare licensing are still to be confirmed.

06 · FUEL AND CONVENIENCE OPTIONS

Three ways to handle fuel

The frontage on Sycamore View is the preferred spot. The Summer Avenue pad fits a station more easily, and dropping fuel removes the most risk. All three were tested with the same module sizes.

Frontage · preferred

Pumps on the road, store beside them

Canopy:
72 × 40 ft, 3 islands, 6 fueling positions, plus a 40 × 20 ft tank reservation. It sits about 91 ft from Sycamore View, fed straight off the existing driveway.
Store:
4,000 SF (80 × 50 ft), 41 ft from the canopy and facing it.
Why split:
The frontage has only about 69 ft of its own road edge. A store and pumps in one block would sit 200–240 ft back from the road.
Watch:
queues at the shared driveway during events, tanker turning paths, and the former 1981 quick-lube pad, demolished with its footings under a 2024 permit (needs an environmental check, called a Phase I).

5800 Summer · fallback

The whole station on one pad

Fit:
the 4,000 SF store, 6-position canopy and aisles fit as one block on the 5800 Summer pad, about 87 ft from Summer Ave.
Road:
Summer Ave / US 70, 21,290 vehicles per day, with about 240 ft of frontage across both pads.
Access:
Uses the existing Summer Ave drive, which stays clear for venue traffic.
Road work:
TDOT is widening Summer Avenue and replacing the Fletcher Creek bridge through mid-2028.
Trade-off:
it uses pad and south event parking, and takes the best pad away from other income. The floodplain edge lies just to the west.

No fuel

Store inside the building, no pumps

The 4,000 SF convenience store moves into the retail program, taken from its 35,000 SF rather than added to it. No new building goes up on the frontage.

  • Least capital and fewest approvals of the three options
  • The frontage stays free for signage, landscaping or another pad use
  • Gives up fuel income and the road-front convenience draw

07 · LEGAL & REGULATORY

Two issues decide the plan

They are flood exposure, and whether CMU-3 zoning allows event halls, fuel sales and supervised childcare. Both can be answered early and cheaply.

FLOOD · THE FIRST THING TO SETTLE

About 74% of the building lies in the 100-year floodplain, but the ground along most of its walls sits 2–4 ft above the flood level.

Under federal flood rules (44 CFR 59.1), if the work costs 50% or more of what the building (not the land) is worth, the whole building must meet today's flood standards. At the county assessor's 2026 building value, that line is about $1.5M of building work, and a full event conversion will very likely cross it.

FEMA's 100-year flood level here (the base flood elevation, or BFE) is 248 ft above sea level. Laser elevation data from the U.S. Geological Survey shows the ground at 250–252 ft along about 85% of the walls, but 247.7–248.8 ft at the rear truck docks. FEMA takes a building off the flood map by letter (a LOMA) only if the lowest ground touching it is at or above the flood level, so that is uncertain here. More likely, a survey will show the floor already meets the City's rule of 250 ft, two feet above the flood level. A surveyor's elevation certificate settles it.

Confirmed from records

  • Commercial mixed-use zoning (CMU-3) on three of the four parcels, per county maps; frontage parcel to confirm. Residential and farm zoning behind the building
  • FEMA’s 100-year flood zone (AE) on map panel 47157C0311G (effective 6 Feb 2013), flood level 248 ft; lenders will require flood insurance
  • 11.72 ac across the four parcels (county assessor 2026), matching the broker; the county map outlines add up to 11.88 ac
  • The frontage is a separate 0.362 ac parcel that any purchase must name explicitly
  • A 1981 quick-lube stood on the frontage until its demolition, footings included, under a 2024 permit; it needs an environmental check (Phase I)

Found, not yet verified

  • Separate parcel numbers alone do not prove the pads can be sold separately
  • Shared access and parking agreements likely transfer with title
  • Underground fuel tanks fall under the Tennessee underground storage tank program, which requires notice 15 days before installation
  • Summer Ave is US-70; driveway changes likely need a state permit
  • The site is very likely inside the Fletcher Creek Overlay, an extra layer of city rules: new buildings need floors 30 inches above the flood level, and the site must hold back storm water on its own land
  • Turning an office into a place where crowds gather triggers today’s fire, exit, restroom and accessibility (ADA) rules

Open questions

  • Are event halls, fuel sales and childcare permitted in CMU-3? A zoning verification letter settles it.
  • Does supervised care of guests' children need a state child care license?
  • Can the public play center offer restricted-access sessions, such as family-only or private-group hours?
  • What parking minimums apply, and how many stalls really exist?

08 · DUE DILIGENCE

What closes the gaps

The property is offered by Ten-X online auction on 2–4 November 2026. It sells as-is, with a 10% deposit and about a 30-day close, and the auction terms give no right to walk away after checking. Finish these checks before any binding commitment. Flood and zoning come first because they decide whether the plan works.

  1. 01Full boundary and ground-level survey (ALTA), settling the acreage and the frontage line
  2. 02Title report, recorded plats, easements and any shared access or parking agreements
  3. 03Surveyor’s elevation certificate recording the floor and the lowest ground touching the building, plus a request to FEMA to take it off the flood map (LOMA) only if that ground is at or above the 248 ft flood level (doubtful at the rear docks)
  4. 04Meeting with the Memphis floodplain administrator on how the 50% rule is measured, and whether the Fletcher Creek Overlay (UDC 8.9) applies
  5. 05Zoning verification letter for every planned use and parcel
  6. 06Phase I environmental report (a check of records and the site for past contamination), covering the former quick-lube
  7. 07Roof, structure, mechanical, electrical and plumbing inspection, including measured columns, ceiling heights, the sprinkler system and a structural engineer's price for removing the four main-room columns, and whether the earthquake code applies to the new event use (it can if the large rooms together hold more than 2,500 people)
  8. 08A fuel company’s plan and written offer; contractor bids to replace the estimated costs

TERMS EXPLAINED

What the technical words mean

SF, ac
Square feet and acres. One acre is 43,560 SF.
Zoning, CMU-3
Zoning decides which uses are allowed on a lot. CMU-3 is the Memphis commercial mixed-use district these parcels are in.
100-year floodplain, FEMA Zone AE
Land with a 1% chance of flooding in any year, as mapped by FEMA. Federally backed loans there require flood insurance.
50% rule (substantial improvement)
If the work costs 50% or more of the building’s value, the whole building must meet current flood rules.
Base flood elevation (BFE)
The water level FEMA maps for the flood with a 1% chance in any year. Here it is 248 ft above sea level (NAVD88).
Elevation certificate, LOMA
A surveyor’s record of the building’s elevations, including the lowest ground touching it (the lowest adjacent grade). If that ground is at or above the base flood elevation, FEMA can issue a Letter of Map Amendment (LOMA) taking the building out of the flood zone.
Due diligence
The checks a buyer makes before committing to buy, such as survey, title, environmental and zoning.
ALTA survey
A detailed boundary survey of the kind title companies and lenders rely on.
Phase I
An environmental review of records and the site, looking for past contamination.
Transfer girder
A long steel beam that carries the roof where a column is taken out, so a room can be column-free.
C-store, QSR
A convenience store, and a quick-service (fast-food) restaurant.
DXF, GLB, BLEND
Technical file types: a CAD drawing, a 3D model and a Blender scene. They need special software to open.
