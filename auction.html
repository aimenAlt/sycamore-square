BEFORE AND DURING THE AUCTION · OCTOBER 2026

What to check before bidding

The property sells by online auction on 2–4 November 2026. It sells as-is, with a 10% deposit and about a 30-day close, and the auction terms give no right to back out after checking. Anything that could change the plan has to be answered before bidding. This page lists what to find out first, which inspections to book, what to ask the City, and what to watch on the auction days.

A checklist from public records and desk research, not legal, engineering or investment advice. Details and sources are on the Legal findings and Feasibility pages and in the Engineering and City package (PDF).

01

Four questions that decide the plan

Answer these first. A wrong answer on any of them changes the plan or the price that makes sense.

1

Is the floor above the flood level?

About 74% of the building is in FEMA's 100-year flood zone. Laser elevation data puts the ground at 250–252 ft along about 85% of the walls, but 247.7–248.8 ft at the rear truck docks; the flood level is 248 ft and the City's rule is 250 ft. If the floor is below 250 ft, flood work and insurance costs rise sharply.

Check: surveyor's elevation certificate and boundary survey.

2

Does the zoning allow every planned use?

Three of the four parcels are zoned CMU-3 (commercial mixed use). No source yet confirms that event halls, fuel sales or supervised childcare (the playground's private zone) are allowed there, and the frontage parcel's zoning needs confirming.

Check: a zoning verification letter from the City for every use and parcel.

3

Can the two middle columns in each hall come out, and does the earthquake code apply?

The plan removes 4 roof columns and adds steel beams, and puts a movable wall in each main room. If the large rooms together hold more than 2,500 people, the building code can require earthquake strengthening of the whole building; capping each hall's posted capacity is the likely fix.

Check: a one-day structural engineer's walkthrough.

4

Is everything the plan needs in the sale?

The plan uses four parcels: 088066 00027, 00020, 00021 and 00024. The frontage and 5810 Summer are also marketed on their own, and the broker's site line crosses a neighbor's lot.

Check: a title report, recorded plats, easements and any shared access or parking agreements.

03

Questions for the City

Ask for one pre-application meeting with planning, the floodplain administrator, fire and building staff.

  • Are event halls, indoor recreation, supervised childcare and fuel sales allowed on each parcel, and what approvals do they need?
  • How is the 50% flood rule measured for this building, and does the Fletcher Creek Overlay apply?
  • What capacities, exits and restrooms do the halls need, and does the earthquake code apply to the new use?
  • How is event parking counted, and can lots be shared between uses?
  • How does the Summer Avenue widening (TDOT, mid-2025 to mid-2028) affect the driveways?

04

Demand and cost checks

  • Line up early interest from caterers, wedding planners and quinceañera, church, corporate and community organizers.
  • Get trade quotes for the biggest items: roof, rooftop units, column removal, the movable walls and the kitchens.
  • Talk to fuel companies and an indoor-playground operator about their terms.
  • Confirm opening hall rates against comparable Memphis halls (see Feasibility).

05

On the auction days

  1. 01Read the purchase agreement and auction terms before registering. They are not negotiable, and the sale is as-is.
  2. 02Confirm exactly which parcels are in the lot being bid on.
  3. 03Have the 10% deposit and the plan for closing about 30 days later ready before bidding.
  4. 04Agree the group's limits before the auction opens, and keep to them.
  5. 05After the sale there is no inspection period: anything not checked becomes the buyer's risk.

06

Red flags that would change the plan

  • The floor is below 250 ft and there is no practical fix.
  • The zoning does not allow event halls on the main parcel.
  • The structural answer makes the column removal far more expensive, or the earthquake code applies to the whole building.
  • A title problem with any of the four parcels or the shared access.
  • Contamination under the former oil-change shop.

07

Two weeks to the auction

  1. This weekorder the survey, the elevation certificate, title and the Phase I; book the structural walkthrough and the trade inspections; ask the City for the meeting.
  2. Next weekcollect the reports, trade quotes and City answers, and update the feasibility numbers.
  3. 2–4 Novemberbid only if the four questions above have good answers.
